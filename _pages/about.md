## Research Interests

My research focuses on stochastic optimization, quantile regression, and modern machine learning, particularly the theoretical foundations and methodology of their uncertainty quantification. Algorithms such as stochastic gradient descent (SGD) are now standard tools for fitting models to large-scale and streaming data, and quantifying the uncertainty of their outputs calls for new theory and methods. My work develops asymptotic and non-asymptotic distributional theory for SGD, covering general learning-rate schedules and heavy-tailed gradients, along with online methods for covariance estimation, confidence intervals, and quantile regression. I am also interested in causal inference and single-cell clustering.

## Bio

I am a PhD candidate in Statistics at the University of Chicago, advised by Professor Wei Biao Wu, and I received my master's degree from the same department. Before coming to Chicago, I earned my bachelor's degree from the School of Mathematical Sciences at Fudan University, where I was advised by Professor Shuqin Zhang. In the summer of 2019, I was a student research intern at Harvard University, advised by Professor Junwei Lu.
